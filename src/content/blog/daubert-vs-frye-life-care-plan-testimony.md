<p class="intro-definition">Whether a life care plan reaches the jury often depends on the admissibility standard in the jurisdiction where the case is filed: Daubert, which asks a judge to assess the reliability of the expert's methodology, or Frye, which asks whether the method is generally accepted in the relevant scientific community.</p>

## The two standards in brief

**Daubert** governs in federal court through Federal Rule of Evidence 702 and has been adopted by a majority of states. The trial judge acts as a gatekeeper and evaluates whether the expert's testimony rests on a reliable foundation and is relevant to the case. Courts commonly consider whether the method has been tested, subjected to peer review, has a known error rate, and is generally accepted. A 2023 amendment to Rule 702 clarifies that the party offering the expert must show it is more likely than not that the testimony meets the rule's requirements.

**Frye** asks a narrower question: is the technique or methodology generally accepted by the relevant scientific community? States including California, Illinois, New York, Pennsylvania, and Washington have historically applied Frye or a Frye-based test, though some apply it only to novel science.

Several states have moved between standards in recent years, and some apply their own variations. Always confirm the current rule in the forum before relying on a summary like this one.

## Why it matters for a life care plan

A life care plan combines clinical judgment with cost projection, so both halves can be challenged:

- **Clinical foundation.** The projected future care must be tied to a medical opinion held to a reasonable degree of medical probability. A plan authored or reviewed by a physician in the relevant specialty, such as Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, is often easier to defend than one built on a nurse planner's recommendations alone.
- **Cost methodology.** Cost figures should come from documented, reproducible sources, such as geographically adjusted billing data, along with a stated approach to inflation and discounting.
- **Fit to the case.** Each line item should trace to the patient's records, diagnoses, and treating-provider input rather than a generic template.

## Questions to ask before retaining an expert

1. Has the expert's life care plan methodology been accepted or challenged in this jurisdiction?
2. Is the expert qualified in the clinical area at issue, and are those credentials current?
3. Can the expert document the sources behind every cost projection?
4. Is the expert's fee structure a flat or hourly fair-market-value rate rather than contingent on the outcome?
